The Bottom Line

We finished August at 55.3% of target (12,848 bags), 10,384 bags short. Weekly output averaged about 2,570 bags — under the 5,808-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (9 bags) only nudged the forecast to 74.59%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 5,808-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~3,238-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~1,619 bags/week) across the month adds ~6,476 bags — lifting achievement from 55.3% to ~83.2%.

Assumes ~1,619 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 16,190,000 of recovered sales.

The Bottom Line

The 7 new products (AMORA, IMANI, LAMORA, LOOP BP, NALA, TAJI, ZULA) reached only 30.8% of their 930-bag target (286 sold) — a 644-bag deficit.

The genuine markdowns (School bag -13%, Kai -4%, Double Press -12%) were the mid/low-volume bags. So the offer price wasn't what pulled buyers on the bulk of sales.

On offer?

Actually on offer (price truly cut): School bag (-13% price, own pace +562%); Kai (-4% price, own pace +65%); Double Press (-12% price, own pace +212%). The cut sold School bag and Kai and Double Press faster than before the offer.

Stock

Season

The Bottom Line

Across 10 combos and 10 power deals (Kenya), the two offer types cleared stock very differently: combos moved 2,081 bags (KES 9,474,019) while power deals moved 2,690 bags (KES 5,166,300). Power deals shifted the most stock; combos made the most money.

The Insight

It's a price-point story: combos averaged KES 4,399 per offer vs power deals KES 1,967. The cheaper power deals clear more volume, so dead stock moves fastest through power deals — yet 4,652 bags remain in Kenya offer stock (plus 660 Sinza, 515 Uganda), so the mix still isn't drawing inventory down fast enough.

Sinza & Uganda offers

Sinza ran 10 combos, 10 singles and 0 specials, moving 162 bags (value 635,799) against 660 in stock. Uganda ran 16 combos and 0 singles, moving 61 bags (value 3,971,000) against 515 in stock. Both are clearing only a thin slice of their offer stock — the same dead-stock problem as Kenya, at smaller scale.

Dead stock or marketing? Sinza moved 162 bags — 24.5% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 15.3%. The bigger lever was the offer/price clearing dead stock — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck. Uganda moved 61 bags — 11.8% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 9.6%. The bigger lever was the offer/price clearing dead stock — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck.

Recommendation
StartRouting the slowest dead stock through power deals (the higher-volume clearer) and keeping premium movers in combos for margin.
StopRestocking the slowest-moving colours until the 4,652-bag Kenya position draws down.
Business Impact

Clearing just 20% of the 4,652-bag Kenya position (~930 bags) in the month is ~4.0% of target — and frees working capital tied up in slow stock.

Assumes a 20% draw-down of the reported Kenya offer stock; excludes Sinza/Uganda upside.
