The Bottom Line

We finished August at 21.6% of target (5,015 bags), 18,217 bags short. Weekly output averaged about 1,672 bags — under the 5,808-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (312 bags) only nudged the forecast to 78.98%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 5,808-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~4,136-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~2,068 bags/week) across the month adds ~8,273 bags — lifting achievement from 21.6% to ~57.2%.

Assumes ~2,068 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 20,681,667 of recovered sales.

The Bottom Line

The 7 new products (AMORA, IMANI, LAMORA, LOOP BP, NALA, TAJI, ZULA) reached only 12.7% of their 930-bag target (118 sold) — a 812-bag deficit.

The Insight

New products received 219 marketing posts but converted just 118 bags, and almost all of it (126 of 118) was Kenya — outside-Kenya is effectively untested (3 bags). Awareness is being created; conversion and distribution are the bottleneck.

Recommendation
StartA concentrated push on the weakest launches with price/offer support to clear the 812-bag deficit.
Start testingOutside-Kenya distribution for the best-performing new lines — 3 bags sold there all month is an untested market, not a dead one.
Business Impact

Recovering half the deficit (~406 bags) is realistic within one cycle; a working outside-Kenya channel would add a comparable second stream.

Assumes weak-launch recovery of 50% of the 812-bag deficit; outside-Kenya upside sized off the 219-post base.

The Bottom Line

Across 10 combos and 10 power deals (Kenya), the two offer types cleared stock very differently: combos moved 893 bags (KES 3,983,207) while power deals moved 1,100 bags (KES 2,372,300). Power deals shifted the most stock; combos made the most money.

The Insight

It's a price-point story: combos averaged KES 4,399 per offer vs power deals KES 1,970. The cheaper power deals clear more volume, so dead stock moves fastest through power deals — yet 5,075 bags remain in Kenya offer stock (plus 541 Sinza, 674 Uganda), so the mix still isn't drawing inventory down fast enough.

Sinza & Uganda offers

Sinza ran 10 combos, 10 singles and 0 specials, moving 80 bags (value 367,939) against 541 in stock. Uganda ran 16 combos and 0 singles, moving 16 bags (value 1,211,500) against 674 in stock. Both are clearing only a thin slice of their offer stock — the same dead-stock problem as Kenya, at smaller scale.

Dead stock or marketing? Sinza moved 80 bags — 14.8% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 7.1%. The bigger lever was the offer/price clearing dead stock — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck. Uganda moved 16 bags — 2.4% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 2.4%. The bigger lever was marketing posting — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck.

Recommendation
StartRouting the slowest dead stock through power deals (the higher-volume clearer) and keeping premium movers in combos for margin.
StopRestocking the slowest-moving colours until the 5,075-bag Kenya position draws down.
Business Impact

Clearing just 20% of the 5,075-bag Kenya position (~1,015 bags) in the month is ~4.4% of target — and frees working capital tied up in slow stock.

Assumes a 20% draw-down of the reported Kenya offer stock; excludes Sinza/Uganda upside.

The Insight

Where we post, we sell: monthly sales on posted bags (1,021) ran ~2.1× the 484 expected. Yet only 5,075 bags were on offer/posted vs 5,738 not. Regionally the discipline collapses — Kenya 80.2%, Sinza 7.1%, Uganda 2.4% — so the biggest untapped demand is in Uganda and in unmarketed stock.
