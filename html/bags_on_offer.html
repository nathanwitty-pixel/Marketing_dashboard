A combo bag bought on its own is not on offer. Not on offer = every other bag, all three countries. Others = corporate invoices, samples and gift bags.

Where the on-offer money comes from

Counted by how it was sold, each sale once: a running combo counts in Combo sales, one per bag (all three countries); a self-made combo’s bags count one by one, like singles; a Kenya bag sold singly counts in Power Deal (every shop, all month), else Deal of the Week (only at its shops, in its tier’s weeks), else Mid-month offers (sold in a timed offer, or a new product). In Tanzania and Uganda, a single on the market’s singles list, or a new product, is a mid-month offer. Everything else is not on offer, including a combo bag bought on its own (full price). The rows add up to the on-offer total. Bar length is relative to the largest row; the % is the share of on-offer money.

Combo bags: inside combos vs on their own

For every bag in a running combo: units sold inside combos (printed on the combo button, running and self-made combos, refunds taken off) against units sold on its own (every single sale: Power Deal, Deal of the Week or not on offer), for the period picked at the top. The bar shows the share inside combos; the tag says which way it sells more.

Offer type summary

Every sale in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda (in KES) in exactly one offer type. Not on offer and Combos cover all three countries (a Jumbo + Jumbo is 1 combo sale holding 2 bags). Power deals, Deal of wk, Corporate, Samples and Gift bags are Kenya only. Mid-month offers = new products + timed offers (Kenya) and listed singles + new products (Tanzania, Uganda). The Country tab splits each row by country. Category and Tier are for the period picked at the top; a combo’s money is split evenly over its bags. Tiers come from bag_tiers.csv — bags without one sit under No tier.
