The Bottom Line

Across 10 combos and 10 power deals (Kenya), the two offer types cleared stock very differently: combos moved 531 bags (KES 2,309,469) while power deals moved 3,832 bags (KES 8,326,400). Power deals shifted the most stock; power deals made the most money.

The Insight

It's a price-point story: combos averaged KES 4,209 per offer vs power deals KES 2,050. The cheaper power deals clear more volume, so dead stock moves fastest through power deals — yet 3,593 bags remain in Kenya offer stock (plus 1,608 Sinza, 1,102 Uganda), so the mix still isn't drawing inventory down fast enough.

Sinza & Uganda offers

Sinza ran 10 combos, 10 singles and 0 specials, moving 82 bags (value 8,946,000) against 1,608 in stock. Uganda ran 5 combos and 12 singles, moving 15 bags (value 1,722,500) against 1,102 in stock. Both are clearing only a thin slice of their offer stock — the same dead-stock problem as Kenya, at smaller scale.

Dead stock or marketing? Sinza moved 82 bags — 5.1% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 60.6%. The bigger lever was marketing posting. Uganda moved 15 bags — 1.4% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 46.0%. The bigger lever was marketing posting — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck.

Recommendation
StartRouting the slowest dead stock through power deals (the higher-volume clearer) and keeping premium movers in combos for margin.
StopRestocking the slowest-moving colours until the 3,593-bag Kenya position draws down.
Business Impact

Clearing just 20% of the 3,593-bag Kenya position (~719 bags) in the month is ~2.2% of target — and frees working capital tied up in slow stock.
