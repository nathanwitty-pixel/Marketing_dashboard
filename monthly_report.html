The Bottom Line

We finished July at 71.8% of target (16,680 bags), 6,552 bags short. Weekly output averaged about 3,270 bags — under the 5,808-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (312 bags) only nudged the forecast to 73.14%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 5,808-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~2,538-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~1,269 bags/week) across the month adds ~5,075 bags — lifting achievement from 71.8% to ~93.6%.

Assumes ~1,269 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 12,687,500 of recovered sales.

The Bottom Line

The 7 new products (AMORA, IMANI, LAMORA, LOOP BP, NALA, TAJI, ZULA) reached only 44.1% of their 930-bag target (410 sold) — a 520-bag deficit.

The Insight

New products received 292 marketing posts but converted just 410 bags, and almost all of it (406 of 410) was Kenya — outside-Kenya is effectively untested (4 bags). Awareness is being created; conversion and distribution are the bottleneck.

Recommendation
StartA concentrated push on the weakest launches with price/offer support to clear the 520-bag deficit.
Start testingOutside-Kenya distribution for the best-performing new lines — 4 bags sold there all month is an untested market, not a dead one.
Business Impact

Recovering half the deficit (~260 bags) is realistic within one cycle; a working outside-Kenya channel would add a comparable second stream.

Assumes weak-launch recovery of 50% of the 520-bag deficit; outside-Kenya upside sized off the 292-post base.

Offer Type Analysis

Each offer — units moved (amber = combo, violet = power deal)
The Bottom Line

Across 10 combos and 10 power deals (Kenya), the two offer types cleared stock very differently: combos moved 2,159 bags (KES 9,344,041) while power deals moved 3,892 bags (KES 7,452,800). Power deals shifted the most stock; combos made the most money.

The Insight

It's a price-point story: combos averaged KES 4,439 per offer vs power deals KES 1,800. The cheaper power deals clear more volume, so dead stock moves fastest through power deals — yet 4,975 bags remain in Kenya offer stock (plus 633 Sinza, 407 Uganda), so the mix still isn't drawing inventory down fast enough.

Sinza & Uganda offers

Sinza ran 10 combos, 10 singles and 0 specials, moving 206 bags (value 983,844) against 633 in stock. Uganda ran 16 combos and 0 singles, moving 89 bags (value 6,550,000) against 407 in stock. Both are clearing only a thin slice of their offer stock — the same dead-stock problem as Kenya, at smaller scale.

Recommendation
StartRouting the slowest dead stock through power deals (the higher-volume clearer) and keeping premium movers in combos for margin.
StopRestocking the slowest-moving colours until the 4,975-bag Kenya position draws down.
Business Impact

Clearing just 20% of the 4,975-bag Kenya position (~995 bags) in the month is ~4.3% of target — and frees working capital tied up in slow stock.

Assumes a 20% draw-down of the reported Kenya offer stock; excludes Sinza/Uganda upside.

The Insight

Where we post, we sell: monthly sales on posted bags (6,925) ran ~3.9× the 1,766 expected. Yet only 4,975 bags were on offer/posted vs 5,602 not. Regionally the discipline collapses — Kenya 89.7%, Sinza 50.7%, Uganda 19.2% — so the biggest untapped demand is in Uganda and in unmarketed stock.

Focus — move the bags NOT on offer

Marketing's real target is the stock sitting not on offer. Read across the three posting lenses:
