The Bottom Line

So far in September we're at 29.6% of target (7,241 bags), 17,227 bags still to go. Weekly output has averaged about 3,620 bags — under the 6,117-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (71 bags) only nudged the forecast to 81.50%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 6,117-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~2,496-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~1,248 bags/week) across the month adds ~4,993 bags — lifting achievement from 29.6% to ~50.0%.

Assumes ~1,248 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 12,482,500 of recovered sales.

Discount vs demand

Because every bag was cut, the discount and back-to-school timing can't be fully separated — both applied at once. But discount depth didn't decide the winners: the deepest cut, Vanity (-27%), sold only 0, while Reo Travel led with 8 on a shallower -11% cut. The cut opened the door, but demand — which bags people wanted for school — chose what actually moved.

Stock

Mostly backpacks with real inventory behind them — 3,035 still in Kenya stock even after the push (led by Double Press at 303). The offer draws down a genuine stock position, not a token one.

Season

All six sit in Baby Bag, Backpack, Briefcase, Chest Bag, Handbag, Lunch Bag, Make Up, Man Bag, School Bag, Sling, Sport, Thigh Bag, Travel, Waist Bag, Washbag — school categories — and the step-up landed in the offer week as schools opened. Reo Travel and Cosmo even sold on zero marketing posts (pure demand). The timing fits the sales pattern as much as the price cut does.

Good move. All six ran a real 6–27% cut and daily sales rose +-37% during back-to-school. The catch: discount depth didn't pick the winners — the deepest cut (Vanity, -27%) sold least, while the popular school lines led. Repeat the timing; you can likely trim the deepest cuts on the slow movers without losing volume.
