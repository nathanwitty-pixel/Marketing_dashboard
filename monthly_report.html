Current Performance

The Bottom Line

We finished July at 71.8% of target (16,680 bags), 6,552 bags short. Weekly output averaged about 3,270 bags — under the 5,808-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (312 bags) only nudged the forecast to 73.14%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 5,808-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~2,538-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~1,269 bags/week) across the month adds ~5,075 bags — lifting achievement from 71.8% to ~93.6%.

Assumes ~1,269 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 12,687,500 of recovered sales.

Posting Yields (Sales from Accurate Posting)

The Bottom Line

Marketing posting is our most effective lever — Kenya posted bags hit 89.7% of expected sales monthly — but 53.0% of in-stock bags (5,602) were never posted. The target gap is sitting in unmarketed stock.

The Insight

Where we post, we sell: monthly sales on posted bags (6,925) ran ~3.9× the 1,766 expected. Yet only 4,975 bags were on offer/posted vs 5,602 not. Regionally the discipline collapses — Kenya 89.7%, Sinza 52.1%, Uganda 21.2% — so the biggest untapped demand is in Uganda and in unmarketed stock.

Recommendation
StartSystematically posting the 5,602 unmarketed in-stock bags — the single largest, cheapest lever against the 6,552-bag target gap.
Start testingThe Kenya posting playbook in Uganda, where sales-achieved is 21.2% vs Kenya's 89.7%.
StopLetting in-stock bags sit unposted — every unmarketed bag is a bag that reliably does not sell.
Business Impact

If the 5,602 unposted bags converted at even a conservative 20% once marketed, that is ~1,120 bags — enough to close ~17.1% of the entire target gap. Bringing Uganda toward Kenya's rate compounds it further.

Assumes a conservative 20% conversion on newly-posted stock (posted bags ran ~3.9× their expected rate, so this is deliberately cautious).
