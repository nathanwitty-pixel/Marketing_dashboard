The Bottom Line

So far in September we're at 43.7% of target (10,693 bags), 13,775 bags still to go. Weekly output has averaged about 2,674 bags — under the 6,117-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (71 bags) only nudged the forecast to 69.46%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 6,117-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~3,443-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~1,722 bags/week) across the month adds ~6,886 bags — lifting achievement from 43.7% to ~71.8%.

Assumes ~1,722 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 17,216,250 of recovered sales.

Where we post, we sell: monthly sales on posted bags (5,734) ran ~4.0× the 1,422 expected. Yet only 7,793 bags were on offer/posted vs 2,613 not. Regionally the discipline collapses — Kenya 80.2%, Sinza 52.1%, Uganda 35.5% — so the biggest untapped demand is in Uganda and in unmarketed stock.

Focus — move the bags NOT on offer

Marketing's real target is the stock sitting not on offer. Read across the three posting lenses:

CombinedSales vs Expected (Posted & Unposted) — of Kenya's in-stock bags, 7,793 are on offer but 2,613 sit not on offer: dead stock we aren't even putting in front of buyers.
With postingSales with Posting vs Expected — where marketing posted, sales ran ~4.0× expected. Posting demonstrably works, so aiming it at the not-on-offer pile is the lever.
No postingSales with no Posting vs Opportunity if Marketed — the sales team already moved 950 bags with zero marketing; the gap up to the opportunity is what marketing leaves on the table by not posting the not-on-offer stock.

Not-on-offer stock by region: Kenya 2,613, Sinza 216, Uganda 497 — and Sinza (52.1%) and Uganda (35.5%) move it far slower than Kenya (80.2%). Same playbook, all three regions.
