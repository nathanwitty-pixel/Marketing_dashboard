The Bottom Line

So far in September we're at 13.9% of target (3,402 bags), 21,066 bags still to go. Weekly output has averaged about 1,701 bags — under the 6,117-bag weekly floor the target requires.

The Insight

Weekly output ran below the bare minimum needed to reach target most weeks. The shortfall is one of throughput and consistency, not demand — corporate orders (1 bags) only nudged the forecast to 69.54%.

Recommendation
StartManaging to a hard 6,117-bag weekly commit, reviewed every Monday — not a single monthly number reconciled at month-end.
StopTreating the ~4,416-bag weekly gap as normal. Each missed week is unrecoverable against a fixed monthly target.
Business Impact

Closing even half the weekly gap (~2,208 bags/week) across the month adds ~8,832 bags — lifting achievement from 13.9% to ~50.0%.

Assumes ~2,208 bags/week recovered × 4 weeks; at ~KES 2,500/bag that is ≈ KES 22,080,000 of recovered sales.

The Bottom Line

Across 10 combos and 10 power deals (Kenya), the two offer types cleared stock very differently: combos moved 484 bags (KES 0) while power deals moved 941 bags (KES 579,200). Power deals shifted the most stock; power deals made the most money.

The Insight

It's a price-point story: combos averaged KES 0 per offer vs power deals KES 2,025. The cheaper combos clear more volume, so dead stock moves fastest through combos — yet 5,088 bags remain in Kenya offer stock (plus 771 Sinza, 1,284 Uganda), so the mix still isn't drawing inventory down fast enough.

Sinza & Uganda offers

Sinza ran 10 combos, 10 singles and 0 specials, moving 21 bags (value 97,186) against 771 in stock. Uganda ran 3 combos and 0 singles, moving 2 bags (value 280,000) against 1,284 in stock. Both are clearing only a thin slice of their offer stock — the same dead-stock problem as Kenya, at smaller scale.

Dead stock or marketing? Sinza moved 21 bags — 2.7% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 63.3%. The bigger lever was marketing posting. Uganda moved 2 bags — 0.2% of its stock cleared, with posting sales-achieved at 35.3%. The bigger lever was marketing posting — but both are modest, so plenty of dead stock is still stuck.

Recommendation
StartRouting the slowest dead stock through combos (the higher-volume clearer) and keeping premium movers in power deals for margin.
StopRestocking the slowest-moving colours until the 5,088-bag Kenya position draws down.
Business Impact

Clearing just 20% of the 5,088-bag Kenya position (~1,018 bags) in the month is ~4.2% of target — and frees working capital tied up in slow stock.

Assumes a 20% draw-down of the reported Kenya offer stock; excludes Sinza/Uganda upside.

The Bottom Line

Staff-made combos were 46 combos moving 51 units (KES 273,999) — 22% of all combo volume — vs 11 official running combos moving 185 units (KES 800,420). 57 combo requests were logged this month. 1 request(s) were rejected (e.g. CBR/2026/0528 — Wrongly Requested bag).

The Insight

The split also shows up at the till: an official running combo offers a wide colour range for the attendant to pick from (avg 17.7 colour options), while a self-made combo is locked to one specific pairing (avg 2.2) — the same divide the CBR link draws, from the shop floor’s point of view.

The Insight

Where we post, we sell: monthly sales on posted bags (2,922) ran ~6.4× the 457 expected. Yet only 5,088 bags were on offer/posted vs 5,246 not. Regionally the discipline collapses — Kenya 87.4%, Sinza 63.3%, Uganda 35.3% — so the biggest untapped demand is in Uganda and in unmarketed stock.

Focus — move the bags NOT on offer

Marketing's real target is the stock sitting not on offer. Read across the three posting lenses:

CombinedSales vs Expected (Posted & Unposted) — of Kenya's in-stock bags, 5,088 are on offer but 5,246 sit not on offer: dead stock we aren't even putting in front of buyers.
With postingSales with Posting vs Expected — where marketing posted, sales ran ~6.4× expected. Posting demonstrably works, so aiming it at the not-on-offer pile is the lever.
No postingSales with no Posting vs Opportunity if Marketed — the sales team already moved 126 bags with zero marketing; the gap up to the opportunity is what marketing leaves on the table by not posting the not-on-offer stock.

Not-on-offer stock by region: Kenya 5,246, Sinza 403, Uganda 906 — and Sinza (63.3%) and Uganda (35.3%) move it far slower than Kenya (87.4%). Same playbook, all three regions.

StartSystematically posting the 5,246 unmarketed in-stock bags — the single largest, cheapest lever against the 21,066-bag target gap.

If the 5,246 unposted bags converted at even a conservative 20% once marketed, that is ~1,049 bags — enough to close ~5.0% of the entire target gap. Bringing Uganda toward Kenya's rate compounds it further.
